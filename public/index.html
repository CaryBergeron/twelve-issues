The lighthouse came first

Before Maggie, before the missing harbormaster, before any of it, there was a lighthouse I passed on a foggy drive up the coast. The keeper's cottage had one lit window and no road leading to it.

I pulled over and wrote three lines on the back of a gas receipt. Those three lines became the opening of chapter one.

If you have read it, you know why that window matters. If you have not, the fog is waiting.

Questions authors ask

Will it spoil my plot?

No. We plan around your major twists and endings and write the way you would talk to readers who have not finished yet. Tell us anything that is off limits and we will leave it out.

Does it work for nonfiction?

Yes. Devotionals, how-to, history, memoir, and business books all work well. Nonfiction issues lean on key ideas, stories, and practical takeaways from the book.

I write a series. Can it mention my other books?

Yes. List them when you set up your project and the issues will point readers to the rest of the series at natural moments.

Is my manuscript safe?

Your files are read in your own browser and only the text is sent to us. We use it to write your issues and nothing else. It is never used to train AI models, and we delete it on request.

Which email service does it work with?

All of them. Copy an issue into Mailchimp, Kit, MailerLite, Substack, or plain Gmail, or download every issue as Markdown or email-ready HTML.

Do I have to send them monthly?

Monthly is the default, and issues are tied to the season. You can choose weekly or every two weeks when you set up your book.
